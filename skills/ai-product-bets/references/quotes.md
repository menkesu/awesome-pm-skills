# Verified quotes: ai-product-bets

All quotes are verbatim from Lenny's Podcast.

## Forward compatibility: will it get better as models improve?

"let's say Claude 8 comes around. What changes in what users do? And then what does that mean for how you're building today?" — Dianne Penn, Lenny's Podcast (00:34:03)

"Like if we're shipping a feature and it doesn't get 2X better as the model gets 2X smarter, it's probably not a feature we should be shipping." — Nick Turley, Lenny's Podcast (01:04:23)

"The thing I try and remind myself is, the AI models that you're using today is the worst AI model you will ever use for the rest of your life." — Kevin Weil, Lenny's Podcast (01:15:41)

"if you're building and the product that you're building is right on the edge of the capabilities of the models, keep going, because you're doing something right because you give it another couple months and the models are going to be great" — Kevin Weil, Lenny's Podcast (00:31:52)

"You have to understand that your differentiator is something that will continue to be there as the LLMs get better and smarter." — Tamar Yehoshua, Lenny's Podcast (01:05:34)

## How far ahead to build

"don't build for today, build for six months from now, build for a year from now. And the things that aren't quite working that are working 20% of the time, will start working 100% of the time." — Benjamin Mann, Lenny's Podcast (01:05:26)

"your product market fit won't be very good for the first six months. But if you build for the model six months out, when that model comes out, you're just going to hit the ground running" — Boris Cherny, Lenny's Podcast (01:06:56)

"You fail if you build for where the models are now, you fail if you build for where you think the models will be in a year. Both outcomes are equally wrong." — Tara Seshan, Lenny's Podcast (00:27:27)

"It's kind of very easy to build the product for the super AGI strong model. I think the hard thing is figuring out for the current model, how do you elicit the maximum capability?" — Cat Wu, Lenny's Podcast (00:51:35)

"I am very confident that the Codex app that we released in February, if that had been ready in November, it would've absolutely failed in the market." — Andrew Ambrosino, Lenny's Podcast (00:33:31)

"So we were too AGI-pilled for the moment." — Andrew Ambrosino, Lenny's Podcast (00:37:32)

## Retesting and shelving

"Let's prototype all of them, decide which things are ready now, and then just let the others sit and bake." — Andrew Ambrosino, Lenny's Podcast (00:32:49)

"It's pretty important to build products that don't necessarily work yet so that you know, okay, what is missing for this product to work?" — Cat Wu, Lenny's Podcast (01:04:52)

"We actually tried building Bolt almost exactly a year ago, with the frontier models at the time. Spent a week or two building it. It just didn't work." — Eric Simons, Lenny's Podcast (01:06:58)

## Scaffolding and model strategy

"we actually do this every time we launch a model, we read through the entire system prompt and we reflect on, okay, for each of these sections, does the model really need this reminder anymore, and if not, we'll remove it." — Cat Wu, Lenny's Podcast (01:02:44)

"maybe scaffolding can improve performance maybe 10%, 20%, something like this, but often these gains just get wiped out with the next model." — Boris Cherny, Lenny's Podcast (01:05:29)

"how could we pull that stuff to today, even if it requires a little bit of engineering on top to make it work or a lot of engineering on top to make it work" — Roman Ugarte, Lenny's Podcast (01:13:34)

## Reliability and enterprise reality

"the POCs get to 60 or 70% of the way there, and the human mind goes, oh, the rest is no big deal." — Jason Droege, Lenny's Podcast (00:38:03)

"these things take 6 to 12 months to get them truly robust enough where an important process can be automated." — Jason Droege, Lenny's Podcast (00:38:03)

"If you have a human process that is 10 or 20% accurate or 10 or 20% liked, AI is awesome." — Jason Droege, Lenny's Podcast (00:32:31)

"if someone's selling you one click-agents, it's pure marketing." — Aishwarya Naresh Reganti, Lenny's Podcast (00:30:31)

"you need to understand the performance of your machine learning to design for it. It needs to be fault tolerant and often you need an escape hatch for the user." — Gustav Söderström, Lenny's Podcast (00:19:03)

"it gives you a mental model and makes you feel in control in the same way that, when you're in a Waymo, you get that screen, for those of you who've tried Waymo." — Nick Turley, Lenny's Podcast (00:13:15)

## Moats and where to play

"if you're selling the shovels like OpenAI and Google with Gemini, you can make money. And if you own the mine, like Rippling, with the data, you can make money." — Matt MacInnis, Lenny's Podcast (01:18:49)

"We're not going to launch an AI sales agent. That's just not what we're building towards." — Logan Kilpatrick, Lenny's Podcast (00:11:17)
