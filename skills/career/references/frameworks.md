# Career: Frameworks Reference

Every framework used in the `career` skill, grouped by theme. Attributions are to Lenny's Podcast guests; where several guests endorse the same idea they are noted. Example phrasings are in italics.

---

## 1. Finding direction and fit

**1. Energy audit (red/yellow/green)** (Nickey Skarstad; Bob Moesta; Paul Millerd) - Decide whether a role fits and what to optimize next.
- Steps: after each meeting or project, color it red (bored/stressed), yellow (baseline) or green (energizing) -> review several weeks -> list what kinds of work are green -> choose the next role to maximize it.
- Variants: Moesta lists 2-3 moments where you gained 2-4x the energy you went in with, and asks why each drain happened (new to it, done it 1,000 times, wrong culture); reflect over about two weeks so memories surface. Millerd: during time off, note after each activity whether it gave or drained energy.
- Fails when: you score without being honest, or in a role so new that everything drains.

**2. Four quests (pushes and pulls)** (Bob Moesta, from 1,000+ switch interviews) - Diagnose why you want to move.
- The four: Get out, Take the next step, Regain control, Realign to what you are good at.
- Steps: list pushes (bored, disrespected, stretched beyond ability) -> list pulls (team that has my back, my best work, time at home) -> map to a quest -> set what the next job must optimize.
- Rule: people leave only when about 3-4 pushes stack up and a pull shows direction; a push alone makes you a complainer. Stated reasons are unreliable: 53% of people who said they left for more money did not get more.

**3. Job features vs job experiences** (Bob Moesta) - Title and salary are static features; experiences keep you. Ask what each feature does for you (money is often respect, bills, or falling behind) and evaluate offers on experiences.

**4. Three layers of language** (Bob Moesta) - Surface preference -> why -> a specific good day and bad day -> the cause that generalizes to other contexts. Turns energizers into transferable job requirements.

**5. Jobcation** (Bob Moesta) - For a 'get out' (burnout) quest only: take a deliberately low-demand, time-limited job to recover before choosing the next real move. Stops working once you need challenge.

**6. Prototype jobs wide, then narrow** (Bob Moesta) - Hypothesize many roles using your strengths, do 10-15 informational interviews (they also rehearse your story), ask people what the job is really like and what was on their resume, narrow to one target.

**7. Three-factor job choice: learning, strengths, fun** (Mayur Kamat) - For any option ask: does it compound my learning, overlap my strengths, will I have fun? Ignore compensation early (about 90% of lifetime exec earnings land in the last five years). Fails when financial constraints dominate. Companion tests: superpower overlap (yours vs the company's), and self-calibration on which PM activities energize or drain you.

**8. Genie, nine lives and direction questions** (Graham Weaver) - Imagine a genie guarantees that whatever you commit your career to will work out but take longer and be harder than you think. Write the answer. Then list nine lives you would be excited to live starting today, pull the most energizing piece into your current life. Questions: what would you do if you knew you would not fail, if you did not need money, what is play for you that is work for others, what are you too embarrassed to say, who do you admire, what will you be best in the world at in 5-10 years. Also spot the option you are talking yourself out of.

**9. Laddering your goal** (Ken Norton) - Ask why you want the goal, and what is important about that, until the answer comes from your values rather than 'because it is there'. Also unpack past roles for the specific elements you loved (e.g. time to grow people, which a team of 35 removed).

**10. Three-overlap career test** (Benedict Evans) - Skills you have, jobs those skills make you good at, what people pay for. Get at least two of three, ideally all. Similar: Jonathan Lowenhar's good at it / like it / market cares.

**11. Strengths discovery** (Jules Walter; Mayur Kamat; Bob Moesta) - Ask what many people say you are good at that you think is no big deal; find the underlying resource and its shadow side. Tools: CliftonStrengths (read the bottom five as likely energy drains), or Dalio's two-layer assessment (self-rating vs your leadership team's; the overlap is your real strength). Endorsed contrarian line: lean into strengths (Sriram Krishnan, Mayur Kamat); only fix weaknesses that actively hold you back.

**12. Impact, execution, optics levels** (Shreyas Doshi) - Product work happens at three levels. Identify your happy level, audit your week, and note that leaders of roughly 50+ person orgs must spend a lot of time on optics. If you are stuck in a non-default level, change scope or role rather than push through.

## 2. Planning the arc

**13. PM your career like a product / career spec** (Deb Liu; Nikhyl Singhal; Gibson Biddle) - Write a spec: success, milestones, skills, metrics, five-year destination. Gibson Biddle: treat the career as hypotheses and small experiments; start with a bad first version.

**14. The Skip** (Nikhyl Singhal) - Plan the move after the next. Define the end state and the job after next, work backwards to what the current and next roles must teach; stay in an ambiguous situation if it builds the skill. In the AI transition: pick roles that save the skip while you gain builder skills. Companion: write the skip-job story in the first person at 6, 12 and 24 months.

**15. Pick a lane among five ambiguities** (Nikhyl Singhal) - Craft, market, organizational, domain/technical, team (growth is another lane). Get world-class at one early and build a story for the next employer. Mastery in one ambiguity also feeds the senior-IC track.

**16. Explore and exploit** (Ada Chen Rekhi) - Early on, run each role as a hypothesis; once rich, tell your manager what you want to learn and optimize for skills over title. She went from entry sales at Microsoft to SVP marketing at SurveyMonkey in about seven years by stacking founder, growth, subscription and marketing experience.

**17. PM learning loop** (Fareed Mosavat) - Execute, generalize (write a transferable hypothesis after each project), communicate, scale to more unknowns, execute again. Impact = work x ability to show it.

**18. Work backwards from a goal, not a title** (Lauren Ipsen; Deb Liu; Casey Winters) - Sequence roles toward the goal (for CEO: product leader, then GM with a P&L, then COO/CEO). Deb Liu: name a distant goal, estimate the timeline, find people who hold the role, break out the first step. Do not let title or valuation bubbles pull you out of a role where you learn.

**19. Ladder vs map; balance with tenure** (Jason Shah) - Ladder is influence and title, map is going where it is interesting. Treat a career as several 5-10 year rounds; stay long enough for institutional knowledge. Warning: most moves are decided on about 10 hours of signal.

**20. Compound learning; give passion six months** (Boz Bosworth; Peter Deng; Mayur Kamat) - Move when you are not learning; pick roles deep enough to keep learning for years; try an unfamiliar assignment about six months before judging passion. Maggie Crowley counterpoint: nearly four years at one company showed her two or three product cycles.

**21. Follow the people / little bets** (Tamar Yehoshua; Sanchan Saxena; Raaz Herzberg) - Skip the five-year plan; follow the best product thinker, engineer or salesperson; take little bets and build rapid recovery. Skills cannot be taken away if the company fails.

**22. Career optionality; portfolio career** (Elena Verna; Fareed Mosavat) - Ask yearly whether staying or moving increases your option pool. Earn depth in full-time jobs first, then test freelance, fractional, advising, courses. Mosavat: be narrow enough to credibly be one of the five best at something you have generalized.

**23. IC vs management vs executive** (Jackie Bavaro; Keith Coleman; Mayur Kamat; Manik Gupta) - Treat management as a two-way door; compare senior-IC pay and impact; sawtooth between large orgs and small new teams. Decide early whether you want C-level (alternatives: founder, domain specialist, balanced life). Manager-to-manager-of-managers is a readiness filter (Gupta).

**24. Product Leader Canyon** (Fareed Mosavat) - Three trip hazards from IC to leader: over-focus on what you are good at, moving from one kind of work to a portfolio, owning the outcome including resources. Also Ethan Evans: what got you here will not get you there (influence and coordination replace details).

**25. Path to CPO / C-suite** (Casey Winters; Carilu Dietrich) - Follow leverage, understand the whole business, optimize for the company, refuse to specialize, treat exec questions as learning. Dietrich: tie work to revenue, speak board terms, learn finance, do tours of duty, pick high-momentum companies. Strategy is the great filter for director-plus.

## 3. Stay or leave; choosing a company

**26. Environment scorecard** (Bangaly Kaba) - Impact = environment x skills. Score manager, resources, team, scope, compensation, culture 0-2 in 0.25 steps yearly; ask whether each can and will change; use the lowest to fix with your manager, change teams or leave. The manager is the variable that moves the rest.

**27. Ex-growth test** (Nikhyl Singhal) - Still searching for product-market fit with a valuation in the hundreds of millions or more? Consider leaving. Stay if the role is the biggest you can get or for bounded loyalty; fear is not a reason.

**28. Stay-or-leave check** (Paige Costello; Ada Chen Rekhi; Bob Moesta) - Learning curve steepness, environment, interest in the problem. Ada's 'frog': check the direction of the temperature. Moesta: employees hire companies; define progress and act when it stalls.

**29. Company vetting** (Carilu Dietrich; Adam Fishman; Austin Hay; Bangaly Kaba) - Dietrich's ten-point list (Rule of 40, investors, recent round, NPS, net dollar retention, growth, burn, market position, Glassdoor); Fishman's people-mission-financials plus observing a leadership meeting and back-channel references; Hay: refusal to share financials with a director-plus candidate is a red flag; Kaba: talk to leavers as well as current staff and triangulate; Meltem Kuran Berkowitz: ask for the team breakdown by function.

**30. Interview your manager** (Melissa Tan; Marty Cagan) - Ask how they develop talent and think about the path for the role; check their background and product quality. The manager who will coach you matters more than the company.

**31. Written career framework** (Farhan Thawar) - Write down what you actually care about, test each offer against it, review yearly.

## 4. Promotion and growth inside a company

**32. Four reasons you are not getting promoted** (Nikhyl Singhal) - No advocate, next role does not exist, impatience, a real development area. Response differs by cause.

**33. The Magic Loop** (Ethan Evans) - Do the job well (as your manager judges it), ask how you can help, do what they ask, then ask for work that serves them and your goal, repeat. Moves from asking to suggesting to acting as trust builds. Works because of reciprocity. Fails with exploitative managers and when you skip the trust-building stage.

**34. Future-framed promotion conversation** (Jackie Bavaro) - *I would like to grow into X at some point; what should I work on now?* Then ask manager peers and long-tenured promoters. Add Julie Zhuo: treat the manager as a guide (ask what success looks like for their job, propose projects that serve the team).

**35. Make the promotion easy to say yes to** (Claire Vo) - State the role; tie it to a company problem (span of control, unmet need); draw the org chart, JD and rollout. Keep career talk small so it does not suck the oxygen out of 1:1s.

**36. What / how / followership** (Manik Gupta) - Promotion readiness is demonstrated end-to-end impact, creating clarity and energy, and smart people choosing to work with you. Inflection point of career correlates with inflection point of product.

**37. Make impact legible** (Deb Liu; Fareed Mosavat; Laura Schaffer; Jeffrey Pfeffer) - Product marketing for yourself; reframe the self-review as educating your manager; teach the org how your area works; build a brand with substance behind it; become the customer-insight person executives lack. Counter-view (Shweta Shrivastava, John Mark Nickels): do not want it too badly, focus on the work.

**38. Sponsors over mentors** (Fareed Mosavat; Chris Miller) - Sponsors trust you with bigger problems and spend capital on you. Work on problems in the company's top four or five priorities.

**39. Mentor playbook** (Jules Walter; Bangaly Kaba; Gibson Biddle; Melissa Tan) - Make the smallest possible ask, apply the advice, circle back with results; pick mentors per skill (good at it and good at explaining it); keep 3-4 mentors on different weeks; find them by telling people your challenge; do not ask someone to be your mentor.

**40. Skill-building routines** (Jules Walter; Vikrama Dhiman; Lane Shackleton) - Time-boxed single-skill sprint (3-6 months, one learning activity a week plus about 3 hours applying it); outcome-backward learning; reverse-engineer great artifacts; one maximum-leverage skill at a time. Shackleton: count your 'oh shit' stretch moments in the last 6, 12, 24 months.

**41. Skill models for self-assessment** (Ravi Mehta; Vikrama Dhiman; Petra Wille; Ian McAllister; Noah Weiss) - Mehta's 12 competencies in four areas; Dhiman's three W's (what you produce, what you bring, operating model) and eight skill axes; Wille's PMwheel; McAllister's top-1% list; Weiss's ten traits. Customize rather than copy.

## 5. Job search, interviews and negotiation

**42. Job Search Council** (Phyl Terry) - 6-8 peers, moderator, first trust meeting; fast seekers twice weekly, slow seekers every two weeks; average search about three months.

**43. Mnookin two-pager** (Phyl Terry) - What you want and do not want in the next role; draft, share, iterate.

**44. Structured listening tour** (Phyl Terry) - Reverse exit interviews, broad network with the golden question, recruiters asked about fit. Creates invested listening posts. Build recruiter relationships before you need them.

**45. Candidate market fit and spear not net** (Phyl Terry) - The market is supply and demand, so fit is set partly by conditions. One sentence with 3-4 attributes. Two-step strategies for stretch targets; consider frontier or IC roles to move to a frontier company.

**46. Job mission with OKRs; present it live** (Phyl Terry) - A private version of the job description as outcomes; share it by call or coffee after a couple of interviews.

**47. Resources before salary** (Phyl Terry) - Agree OKRs, name what you need to hit them, ask for commitment, then talk comp. Refusal is a red flag. Two CPOs example: the one who negotiated a tech-debt budget was promoted; the other was job hunting 18 months later.

**48. Interview as discovery** (Jacob Warwick; Nan Yu) - Treat the interview like a consultative sale: learn their pain, reflect it back, ask live feedback (*what should I have asked that I did not?*), ask the hiring manager's burning problem and OKRs.

**49. Warwick's offer playbook** - Do not name a number early; *what did you have in mind?*; soften with *what is the chance there could be a little more?*; slow down (haste equals risk); negotiate live, not via email or recruiter; never split the difference by reflex; restructure comp when scope creeps; performance and milestone triggers; make them prove stated values; give them a positive reputation to uphold. Benchmarks: about 20% improvement from simple pushback, about 40% with coaching. Contrarian to standard anchor-first advice.

**50. Gives and gets; options; tapering** (Madhavan Ramanujam) - For B2B and founder negotiations: never concede without a get, show up with good-better-best options, taper concessions (15, 5, 2). Useful for a consultant or advisor path.

**51. Resume and story** (Melissa Perri; Matt LeMay; Bob Moesta; Lauren Ipsen) - Lead with problem, users, launch and metric moved; no Scrum jargon; say what you did with a number. Pixar story spine for the career story; practice telling it before you need it. Executives are judged on story and references more than resumes.

**52. Break into PM** (Annie Pearl; Naomi Gleit; Kevin Yien; Christina Wodtke; Maggie Crowley) - Four paths: APM programs, internal junior PM job board, partnering with a PM informally, early-stage startup. Do the job before the title; adjacent roles (engineer, designer, sales, ops) give reps; take any role that stamps you with the PM title.

## 6. The AI transition

**53. Cross the threshold; builders wanted; shadow superpower** (Nikhyl Singhal) - Commit to staying current; information-movers are most exposed; those who mastered the old way have the least incentive to reinvent; brands count for less than how modern your building methods are; swallow your ego for a smaller role at a modern company if needed.

**54. Stacked skills, T-shape, bundle of tasks** (Marc Andreessen) - Two skills are worth more than double, three more than triple; stay deep in one to judge AI output; a job is a bundle of tasks, so manage task loss and task change. Do not be fungible.

**55. Ship weekly; chassis project; ride the model** (Anish Acharya; Dan Shipper; Anton Osika; Ian Silber) - One small shipped thing a week; a throwaway project you iterate with each model; retry old failures on every release; a full week on a real outcome.

**56. Marry outcomes, own outputs** (Andrew Ambrosino; Zevi Arnovitz) - Process churns; commit to the outcomes you uniquely deliver, and own every AI output you present.

**57. Agency, taste, judgment** (Max Schoening; Varun Mohan; Aishwarya Naresh Reganti; Michael Truell) - As skill gaps close, agency and taste differentiate. Build prototypes of your ideas instead of describing them.

**58. Give away your Legos; learn not know** (Molly Graham) - In rapid change hand off what you have mastered to take the bigger pile; expect a six to nine month J-curve fall on a stretch role; ask the dumb questions.

**59. Where code still matters** (Amjad Masad; Marc Andreessen; Scott Wu; Bret Taylor; Guillermo Rauch) - Learn problem-first, prompting, reading code and debugging; systems thinking; vocabulary to steer the model. Opposed by Boris Cherny and Lazar Jovanovic (judgment and taste first).

**60. Career resilience beyond the job** (Paul Millerd; Graham Weaver; Eric Ries) - The default path as a script you opt into; three-month sabbatical; safe side experiments before quitting; ask if the company mission is in the legal charter.
