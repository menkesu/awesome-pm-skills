# Verified Quotes: Customer Interviews

Copied verbatim from the digest. Grouped by theme.

## Forces and jobs
- "if F1 and F2 are not greater than F3 and F4, they're not going to move, they're not going to do anything." — Bob Moesta, Lenny's Podcast (00:12:34)
- "it's not just about pain and gain, it's about context and outcomes." — Bob Moesta, Lenny's Podcast (00:10:34)
- "instead of trying to look for themes across all of them, we actually do something, instead of segmenting them, we cluster them, we find the pathways" — Bob Moesta, Lenny's Podcast (00:19:55)
- "I only talk to people who have already tried to make the progress, right?" — Bob Moesta, Lenny's Podcast (00:28:09)
- "Don't think of users as using your product. Think of users as hiring you to do something for them." — Robby Stein, Lenny's Podcast (00:52:35)
- "The user hires you for a thing, be that user for a second. Would you even buy the thing that you just made?" — Max Schoening, Lenny's Podcast (01:06:23)

## How to interview
- "The real measure is tell me about your behavior. What did you actually do?" — Teresa Torres, Lenny's Podcast (39:57)
- "I actually tell people if your interview feels like you're having a beer with a buddy, that's a good sign." — Teresa Torres, Lenny's Podcast (36:06)
- "People don't really get out of bed for their second problem. They get out of bed for their first problem." — Jeff Weinstein, Lenny's Podcast (00:20:34)
- "please, please, please do not ask questions like what keeps you up at night? If you had a magic wand, what are your pain points today? I can guarantee you that answer changes every single day." — Jen Abel, Lenny's Podcast (00:42:04)
- "The word interesting is a polite way of saying no, right? And so I'm either looking for wow statements or I'm looking for demonstrated behavior that shows interest." — Todd Jackson, Lenny's Podcast (01:18:06)
- "And the best way for you to figure out what is the intensity of the problem is not to ask them but to watch them or to watch them solve the thing that they do." — Gustaf Alstromer, Lenny's Podcast (00:32:15)
- "You can't empathize with the summary. You have to be in the room fully immersed, no phones, just actually hearing the words and the intonation." — Peter Deng, Lenny's Podcast (01:34:48)

## Sample size and cadence
- "it starts to repeat around seven or eight. And I usually do 10, no more than 12. And I would rather do two rounds of 12 interviews than do 24 interviews." — Bob Moesta, Lenny's Podcast (00:27:07)
- "if you talk to enough people and you can predict 70 to 80% of what the next person is going to say to you, because you've just talked to so many people and you've heard the pattern so clearly, that's when you've talked to enough people." — Todd Jackson, Lenny's Podcast (01:24:44)
- "Can I make it so that when you wake up on Monday morning, there's an interview on your calendar and you literally did nothing to get it there?" — Teresa Torres, Lenny's Podcast (26:35)
- "the death of customer discovery is friction. If you ask individuals to do customer discovery, they will not do customer discovery." — Oji Udezue, Lenny's Podcast (00:31:34)

## Research that changes decisions
- "It's work we do to signal to each other how customer obsessed we are, not because we want to make a different decision." — Judd Antin, Lenny's Podcast (00:24:11)
- "The micro research should be much more common. A lot of researchers think that that's scut work, that usability is something junior researchers do. I completely disagree." — Judd Antin, Lenny's Podcast (00:37:55)
- "we end up selectively remembering things and then constructing narratives around them in a way which makes us feel like we already knew that, when we in fact did not." — Judd Antin, Lenny's Podcast (00:43:16)
- "call 10 of them. Pick up the phone yourself. Don't ask a researcher to come up with a plan to go do the research to then summarize it, to then bring it to you, which has so many degrees of distance built in." — Jag Duggal, Lenny's Podcast (00:26:27)
- "PMs need direct exposure to raw material. End of story." — Kevin Yien, Lenny's Podcast (00:54:39)

## Say versus do, and whom to ignore
- "We try to understand what people actually do versus what they say they will do." — Kristen Berman, Lenny's Podcast (07:57)
- "to get to product market fit, you have to deliberately not act on the feedback of many of your early users." — Rahul Vohra, Lenny's Podcast (00:54:07)
- "your friends want to do you a favor, so they're going to try the product. They're also going to lie to you. They're going to tell you how great it is. And then you look at the usage and nobody's coming back." — Grant Lee, Lenny's Podcast (00:23:10)
- "no, user research is a scarce resource. We have to reserve it for the areas that have extreme uncertainty and high leverage for getting to certain" — Casey Winters, Lenny's Podcast (00:11:30)
