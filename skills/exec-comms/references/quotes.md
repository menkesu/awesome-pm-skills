> "you can't cut to the chase unless you know what the chase is. You can't unbury the lead unless you know what the lead is."

## Clarity, density and honesty
> "being concise is not about absolute word count, it's about economy of words. It's about the density of the insight that you're sharing."
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (00:18:31)

> "if everything is bolded then nothing is being highlighted, right?"
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (00:29:51)

> "You can avoid a lot of problems if you speak accurately about your level of conviction and about the actual amount of evidence that you have for something."
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (00:42:41)

> "if you do a pros and cons list, but at the top, you say my recommendation is X, here's pros and cons of that, or here's some risks associated with it or whatever, you're building trust with your reader"
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (42:09)

> "your reader doesn't know which parts are FYIs or which parts are background versus what is the thing that you want their response on"
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (43:04)

> "I think the blast radius of a poorly written memo is way bigger than most people think."
> — Wes Kao, Lenny's Podcast (00:25:37)

> "definitely using numbers more than adjectives. Strict concision"
> — Jason Shah, Lenny's Podcast (00:24:07)

> "It's better sometimes to be wrong but clear than the other way around."
> — Jules Walter, Lenny's Podcast (00:40:36)

## Story, slides and persuasion
> "the audience gets to make a choice if they accept or reject your idea. So, the balance of power is with them and not you."
> — Nancy Duarte, Lenny's Podcast (00:18:48)

> "And you need to set up every talk by stating what is. And then it moves straight up and you move to what could be"
> — Nancy Duarte, Lenny's Podcast (00:26:49)

> "each slide itself should make one point in support of that big idea. People can't process too many things at one time"
> — Nancy Duarte, Lenny's Podcast (00:41:35)

> "Put a page in front of all your decks. It's just boxes. Just get the narrative right."
> — Nancy Duarte, Lenny's Podcast (00:48:18)

> "make the title the takeaway of the slide so that the person looking at it has to do zero work to take away."
> — Andy Raskin, Lenny's Podcast (01:00:41)

> "if you don't have a one thing for each slide, either the slide shouldn't exist or it should be multiple slides."
> — Tristan de Montebello, Lenny's Podcast (01:42:16)

> "if you're not engaged in one of those four things while you're speaking, people are not listening to you anymore."
> — Matthew Dicks, Lenny's Podcast (00:52:00)

> "if you tell people a bunch of facts, they'll forget most of them, especially those that don't fit into their current mental model of how the world works. But if you tell them a story that's full of facts, they will remember it."
> — Christina Wodtke, Lenny's Podcast (00:41:38)
