# Hire: verified quotes

All quotes are verbatim from Lenny's Podcast transcripts.

## Define great before you source

> "Actually, you won't. You won't know because you haven't spent the time objectively identifying what great looks like." — Adam Ward, Lenny's Podcast (00:16:19)

> "you need to get incredibly granular on the front end around, what is this person going to be coming in to do? What's their mandate?" — Lauren Ipsen, Lenny's Podcast (00:14:12)

> "Just because this is the best talent, that doesn't necessarily mean they are the best talent for this role today." — Lauren Ipsen, Lenny's Podcast (00:12:46)

> "I think sometimes people just bring in a head of product to do everything, so that's probably not the best way to go about things. I think that ends up being a unicorn" — Lauren Ipsen, Lenny's Podcast (00:17:08)

> "Start with, it's 12 months later, you hired the person, they started today, 12 months have gone by, you're clinking champagne because of how great it's been." — Jonathan Lowenhar, Lenny's Podcast (00:51:18)

## Sourcing

> "By definition, that is not the top 20%. That is just the 20 people who you caught on a bad day for them. Good day for you, bad day for them." — Adam Ward, Lenny's Podcast (00:11:59)

> "you have to build a company on undiscovered talent. I don't think you really want to compete for the people that everybody else wants." — Keith Rabois, Lenny's Podcast (00:25:06)

> "You hire the person who's reporting to them, the lieutenant." — Gokul Rajaram, Lenny's Podcast (00:36:48)

## Ownership and judgment

> "Can they take an idea and make it happen?" — Keith Rabois, Lenny's Podcast (00:18:59)

> "We hand you a problem. We hand you a rough solution. You figure out the how." — Shishir Mehrotra, Lenny's Podcast (01:09:52)

> "in six months, if I'm telling you what to do, I've hired the wrong person." — Peter Deng, Lenny's Podcast (01:02:03)

> "Those first couple reps have to be people you would buy your own product from. That's it." — Jason Lemkin, Lenny's Podcast (00:15:13)

## Interviews and work samples

> "Interviews are not a good predictor of performance. We know this." — Farhan Thawar, Lenny's Podcast (01:03:28)

> "if you then use your intuition after having done that, not before, that then you actually can really drastically improve your hit rate on hiring. And in that case, it was from about a 50% hit rate to 65." — Annie Duke, Lenny's Podcast (00:22:04)

> "Research has shown that work samples are the highest predictor of success at a company, but yet most companies are still spending a lot of time doing one-to-one across the table assessments." — Adam Ward, Lenny's Podcast (00:45:24)

> "It helps to enforce, do you want to be around this person type test, because you are around this person for two days, a bunch of meals with them." — Michael Truell, Lenny's Podcast (00:56:38)

> "I don't think you're a good PM if you haven't shipped something that's really shitty, you just haven't had enough reps, you haven't done it enough time." — Maggie Crowley, Lenny's Podcast (00:55:59)

> "bring in people who know content and teach them process, as opposed to bringing in people who know process and trying to teach them content, because that's a bloody hard thing to do." — Sanchan Saxena, Lenny's Podcast (00:53:24)

## References

> "Same person, wrong question, wrong result." — Keith Rabois, Lenny's Podcast (00:12:43)

> "back channel references I think will always provide a greater source of truth than provided. It's similar to an Instagram Reel as opposed to the photos you're tagged in." — Lauren Ipsen, Lenny's Podcast (00:49:04)

> "if you put someone through an interview process, you've probably spent what, eight hours total with them? If you talk to people that actually have worked with them before, you're probably benefiting from thousands of hours of direct experience." — David Singleton, Lenny's Podcast (01:10:49)

> "Reference check at the top, work, product and presentation next, and then all the interviews. When they disagree, that's the order we judge." — Shishir Mehrotra, Lenny's Podcast (01:23:23)

> "if it would be a huge mistake if I didn't bring him on, if you think he's amazing, then call me back. Otherwise, don't bother." — Phyl Terry, Lenny's Podcast (01:43:19)

## Decisions, closing and review

> "They're a confidence engine. They're not a decision-making engine." — Adam Ward, Lenny's Podcast (00:28:10)

> "Almost every time we hired the three out of four, the person with the least amount of weaknesses. And we changed it, and we went with the spikier people" — Brian Halligan, Lenny's Podcast (00:07:59)

> "when you get to offer stage, I send an email with no more than eight bullet points and I say all the terrible things that are probably going to reinforce their fears." — Kevin Yien, Lenny's Podcast (00:48:44)

> "So once they're ready to go, you got to capitalize on that. You can't create openings for them to have second guessing." — Adam Ward, Lenny's Podcast (00:52:39)

> "The best people in the world in hiring will tell you they have about a 50% average in terms of being right." — Molly Graham, Lenny's Podcast (01:04:00)

> "hire slow and fire fast. People hire fast and fire slow." — Brian Halligan, Lenny's Podcast (00:09:37)

> "Every time that you hire someone new, mark your calendars for 30 days down the road and ask yourself one question, knowing what I know today, would I hire this person? If the answer is no, fire them immediately." — Uri Levine, Lenny's Podcast (00:00:23)
